# 201809071625 The fluency illusion prevents deep understanding
[[201909021710]] [[201809071652]]

The fluency illusion is when you believe you have a deep understanding of something, but really only have a shallow one [@Young2018, W4].  You have the illusion of explanatory depth.

Ex:  Most people think they know what a bicycle looks like, yet most people cannot draw one.

Re-reading notes contributes to the fluency illusion.  The familiarity makes us think we understand something that we actually can't recall.

We think we undeewrstand because explanations can have varying degrees of depth.  Whereas facts are all or nothing.  It is much easier to self-assess your level of knowledge.  We also have a strong bias to think we understand things.  Self assessment tends to say, "I have a shallow understanding.  That's good enough."

We can combat the fluency illusion with practice; specifically practice at the level of use.  For more conceptual topics, the Feynman technique can also work.
