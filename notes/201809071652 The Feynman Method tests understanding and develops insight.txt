# 201809071652 The Feynman Method tests understanding and develops insight
#learning
[[201805191125]] [[201809071625]] [[201806110744]]

How [@young2019ultralearning, p. 192][@Young2018, W4]:

1) Write down the idea you are trying to understand.
2) Explain it **as if you were teaching it to someone else**.  Breaking it into component parts helps.  Analyze from multiple perspectives.  Try to visualize or use concrete examples.
	- Often you have trouble with insight because you either are missing pieces or can't hold all the pieces in your head because they aren't chunked up yet.
	- Break the idea down.  Build it back up.  Then test it with examples.
3) When you get stuck, go back to your reference material for help.

- It should take roughly 20 minutes to do the Feynman method.

There are 2 variants of the Feynman Technique

- Broad Intuition
	- Ex: I don't understand voltage.
	- If possible, look for analogies or visualizations (ex: Kanban and cars on the highway)
- Mastering Details
	- Ex: Understanding this paragraph
	- Ex: Understanding why these 2 values cancel out in problem 5a
	- Be as specific as possible.  Do not define it too broadly.
	- Here you want to write MORE than what's explained in the textbook.

Additional uses of the Feynman technique:

- Self-testing conceptual knowledge.
- Ideas you don't understand at all.
- Trying to understand technical or abstract ideas.
- Creating deeper intuition.
- Eliminate the fluency illusion
